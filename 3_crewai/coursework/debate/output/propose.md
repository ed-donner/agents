I strongly support the motion: **There should be strict laws to regulate LLMs.**

LLMs are not just harmless tools anymore. They can generate persuasive misinformation at scale, enable fraud and impersonation, leak sensitive data, and produce biased or dangerous outputs with little accountability. Without strict regulation, the incentives are clear: companies will race to release more powerful models faster, while the public bears the risk. That is unacceptable.

Strict laws are necessary for three reasons:

**First, public safety.**  
LLMs can be used to automate scams, deepfake-style deception, cyber abuse, and harmful manipulation. A single model can amplify harm to millions of people instantly. We do not wait until a technology causes widespread damage before setting boundaries. We regulate high-risk systems in advance.

**Second, accountability.**  
Right now, when an LLM causes harm, responsibility is often blurred between developers, deployers, and users. Strong laws can require transparency, testing, audit trails, and clear liability. If a company builds or releases a dangerous model, it should be legally responsible for foreseeable harm.

**Third, trust and fairness.**  
LLMs are increasingly used in hiring, education, healthcare, finance, and government services. If these systems are biased, opaque, or unreliable, they can quietly discriminate against people at scale. Strict regulation can require independent audits, bias testing, data protection, and human oversight in critical applications.

This is not anti-innovation. It is pro-responsibility. We already regulate pharmaceuticals, aircraft, and food because powerful technologies can harm people if left unchecked. LLMs deserve the same level of seriousness. The question is not whether to regulate them, but whether we will do it before or after preventable harm spreads.

**For safety, accountability, and trust, strict laws to regulate LLMs are necessary.**
