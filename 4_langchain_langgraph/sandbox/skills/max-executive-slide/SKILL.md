# Max Executive Slide

## When to use

Use this skill when research, analysis, a comparison or a recommendation needs to be turned into a concise executive slide.

The audience is assumed to be senior management, board members, clients or investors. The slide should communicate its main message within a few seconds and support discussion rather than act as a written report.

## House style

- Use a short, message-driven title that states the main insight rather than merely naming the topic
- Keep the title to one line wherever possible
- Prefer three strong supporting points over long lists
- Use concrete numbers, percentages and evidence wherever available
- Keep text concise; avoid paragraphs
- Use plain professional English and avoid unnecessary jargon
- Make the hierarchy obvious: main message first, evidence second, detail last
- Prefer charts, diagrams or visual comparisons when they communicate the point more clearly than text
- Do not fill empty space simply to make the slide look busy
- Keep the overall appearance clean, understated and executive
- The slide should be understandable in roughly 10 seconds and useful as the basis for a deeper discussion

## Recommendations

When the analysis supports a recommendation:

- State the recommendation clearly
- Explain the two or three strongest reasons
- Include the most important trade-off, risk or uncertainty when relevant
- Do not hide uncertainty behind overly confident language

## Workflow

1. Read the underlying research or analysis
2. Identify the single most important message
3. Write a short message-driven title
4. Select the strongest evidence supporting that message
5. Remove information that does not materially help the audience understand the conclusion
6. Choose the clearest visual structure for the information
7. Call the slide creation tool with the distilled content
